01 · Why a mobile app

The web app runs the operation. The field runs on WhatsApp.

The current system is a full command-and-control suite — 60+ pages, 93 entities, 131 backend functions — and it is genuinely good at the חמ״ל desk. But the moment a case opens, the actual search happens on phones: volunteers get dispatched over WhatsApp, navigate with Waze, photograph evidence with the camera app, and report back by voice message. None of that structured field data flows into the system unless someone at a desk re-types it.

A mobile app closes that loop. Four capabilities the browser fundamentally cannot deliver justify the build on their own:

Push, not pull

Today alerts go out via WhatsApp templates and Pushover. Native push means a new case, a task assignment, or a commander broadcast lands on every relevant phone in seconds — with sound classes for urgency, and deep links straight into the case.

Live location

VolunteerTravelSession already exists in the schema. Background GPS turns it into live force tracking: the commander's map shows every searcher, and sector coverage is recorded automatically instead of reconstructed afterwards.

The camera is an evidence pipeline

A photo taken in-app is geotagged, timestamped, attached to the case and the sector, and synced when signal returns. Same for interview audio — recorded on-device and fed straight into the existing Soniox transcription pipeline.

Works where searches happen

Forests, wadis, parking-garage sub-levels. An offline-first case bundle (subject profile, my tasks, sector maps, contacts) plus an outbox queue means the app never shows a spinner where a life-critical detail should be.

02 · Who holds the phone

Five personas, two of them primary

The permission model already in production (roles, UnitPermissions, case-status visibility, volunteer linking via WhatsApp OTP) maps cleanly onto mobile. The app is built for the first two personas; the rest are served, not designed for.

Primary

Field volunteer · מתנדב שטח

Linked via UserVolunteerLink + WhatsApp OTP. Needs: my task, my sector, navigate, report, photograph, check in/out. One thumb, bright sun or total darkness, possibly gloves.

Primary

Unit / field commander · מפקד יחידה

Runs the event from a vehicle. Needs: force map, task board, ops log, broadcast to everyone, open a case from the field, approve volunteers arriving at staging.

Secondary

Medic · חובש

When the subject is found: quick vitals entry, interventions, medication log — MedicalCareEpisode and its sub-entities, phase 2 "medic mode".

Secondary

Dispatcher / back office · חמ״ל

Stays on the web app — big screens, bulk edits, reports. Mobile gives them read access and notifications only.

Tertiary

Guest / family · אורח

generateGuestAccessToken already supports time-limited viewers. A share-link web view is enough; no native surface in scope.

04 · Mobile superpowers

What only a native app can add

Offline case bundle

On assignment, the app pulls subject card, my tasks, sector geometry, contacts, and map tiles. Mutations queue in an outbox with visible sync state — honest about what has and hasn't reached the חמ״ל.

Background breadcrumbs

Battery-tuned GPS trail during an active session only. Feeds live force map + automatic sector-coverage record.

Geotagged capture

Photos, voice notes, and markers carry location + time by default. Evidence chain-of-custody improves for the police report downstream.

Biometric unlock

Base44 OAuth once, then FaceID/fingerprint. Volunteer OTP linking (sendOtpWhatsapp/verifyOtpWhatsapp) reused as the mobile onboarding flow.

Deep links out

Waze/Apple Maps to coordinates, dialer and WhatsApp to contacts, share-sheet for the subject flyer.

Quiet hours that break

Normal notifications respect the OS; a "subject found" or SOS alert uses critical-alert entitlement (iOS) / full-screen intent (Android) — this is what pagers were for.

05 · Main navigation

Deck — one bar, one gesture

המגירה — טאבים שקטים למטה, וכל פעולות השטח במשיכה אחת למעלה

The chosen direction. Deck keeps the most learnable pattern in mobile — five flat tabs — and adds exactly one idea on top: swipe up anywhere on the bar and a quick-action tray rises, carrying the four actions this user is most likely to need right now. Tabs answer "where do I go"; the tray answers "what do I do" — and it does it in one glove-proof gesture, from any screen, without hunting for a floating button.

Anatomy

The bar

Five destinations, right to left: בית · מפה · יומן · משימות · נעדר. Badge discipline: only יומן ever shows a count — one red number, never a constellation. A 3-pixel sync truth-line runs across the top edge: solid green when everything reached the חמ״ל, amber and partial while the offline outbox drains. Icons dim to amber-on-dark automatically in night mode.

The gesture

The entire bar is the tray handle — a full-width, thumb-height target usable blind, in rain, with gloves. Swipe up: tray springs open with a haptic tick. Flick down or tap outside: it files away. Long-press anywhere on the bar does the same as the swipe, so there are two paths to discover it, plus a one-time coach mark on first run (animated hand, three seconds, never again).

The tray

Four actions, 64pt targets, one row — no scrolling inside a panic surface. Contents come from the user's role on this case via the existing permission graph, and every action works offline: capture and reports queue in the outbox, and the truth-line shows it honestly. The tray never contains navigation — destinations live in tabs, verbs live in the tray, no exceptions.

SOS discipline

SOS sits in the tray only — one swipe + one hold away, impossible to graze. Holding fills a 2-second ring around the button (haptic ramp), then fires: location + critical alert to the commander channel, bypassing silent mode on recipients. A 5-second cancel window follows for false triggers. Slot and behavior are identical for every role.

Edge cases, decided now

No gesture conflicts

The swipe begins on the bar, never on content — so it can never fight map panning, list scrolling, or the OS home gesture (the bar sits above the home indicator inset).

Accessibility

The tray is also a plain button for VoiceOver/TalkBack ("פעולות מהירות"), every action is labeled, and prefers-reduced-motion swaps the spring for a fade.

Keyboard open

Bar and tray yield to the keyboard entirely; no floating remnants over text entry. Dismiss keyboard → bar returns.

Mid-action state

While recording a voice report the tray stays pinned open showing the waveform and a stop button — the gesture surface becomes the recorder.

Case closed

On a closed case the tray collapses to two read-only actions and the bar desaturates — the interface itself says the event is over.

Tab overflow

Never. Five destinations is the hard cap; everything else (contacts, medical, hospitals, settings) lives inside בית as cards. The bar does not grow.

The three-second promise: swipe-up → camera already open → shutter → geotagged finding uploading. Three seconds from "I see something" to evidence in the system — that is the metric Deck is designed around, and the one to test in the first field pilot.

08 · App administration

Managing the app itself

System administration — users, roles, permissions, imports — stays in the web app, as section 03 decided. But shipping a mobile fleet creates an administrative domain that does not exist today: devices, push, versions, remote control. It needs two surfaces — a full console inside the existing web system, and a pocket subset for admins who are also in the field.

Surface 1 — web console: ניהול אפליקציה

A new MobileAdmin page in src/pages, beside SystemAdmin. Visible to admin / is_global_command; unit-scoped view for מפקד יחידה. Every action here is re-authorized server-side and audit-logged — the console renders what the backend permits, never the other way around.

Surface 2 — in-app admin panel PHASE 2

A compact panel inside בית, shown only to admins and unit commanders — the smallest set of actions that cannot wait for a desk:

Approvals inbox

Volunteer link requests (links/approve) and unit assignment requests approved from the phone — at the staging point, minutes after the volunteer arrives, not hours later at a desk. Push-notified, one-tap approve/reject.

Force readiness

For the current case: who has the app installed, last seen, tracking on/off, battery level of active searchers. The commander knows at staging who can actually be reached and tracked.

Emergency controls

Suspend a user (User.is_suspended), push a notice to the unit, resend a volunteer's OTP. Deliberately short list — the phone approves and alerts; it never destroys.

Guardrail: destructive fleet actions — remote revoke, kill switch, version gates, flag changes — live in the web console only. The in-app panel is additive (approve, notify, unblock), so a stolen commander phone can inconvenience the unit but cannot dismantle it.

09 · Architecture

One backend, one new client

Nothing about the Base44 backend changes shape: the app consumes the same entities and the same functions (getDashboardFullData, caseDiscussion, listCasesLite…), with the same authorization discipline (loadCase() + userCanAccessCase() on every case-scoped call).

Framework decision — native, Capacitor, or both-native?

Answer: one React Native + Expo codebase, compiled into two fully native apps. Not Capacitor — a webview cannot deliver the Deck tray's gesture physics, background GPS, offline map tiles, or cold-start speed, and those are the reasons this app exists. Not separate Swift + Kotlin apps either — two native codebases double every feature, every fix, and every regression test for a volunteer-run organization, with no capability this app needs in return. React Native ships real native UI on both platforms from one JS codebase the team already knows.

ApproachVerdictReasoning
React Native + ExpoRECOMMENDEDThe team is React/JS; the Base44 SDK is JavaScript (validate on RN early — worst case, a thin fetch wrapper over the same REST endpoints). Native maps, background location, camera, and push are first-class via Expo modules. EAS Update gives OTA fixes matching Base44's fast-deploy culture. RTL via I18nManager + existing i18next resources reused as-is.
Capacitor (wrap the web app)FALLBACKWeeks to a store build and pixel-identical RTL — but the Deck tray's gesture physics, background GPS, offline maps, and cold-start performance are exactly where webviews disappoint, and those are the reasons this app exists. Acceptable as an interim "v0.5" if push alone must ship early.
Flutter / separate native iOS + AndroidREJECTEDNew language(s), zero code or skill reuse, no SDK reuse — and two native codebases mean double maintenance forever. Nothing here demands either.

New backend surface (small, additive)

3 new entities

DeviceToken — user email, platform, FCM/APNs token, app version, last-seen, revoked.
LocationPing — session ref, batched coordinates, accuracy, battery. (Or extend VolunteerTravelSession with a points array — decide with real payload sizes.)
MobileAppConfig — version policy, feature flags, tray slots per role (section 08).

~8 new functions

registerDeviceToken · push/dispatch (fan-out by case/unit/role, called from existing flows like sendCaseBroadcast) · trackLocationBatch · getOfflineBundle (trimmed dashboard for caching) · checkInAtStaging (QR/geofence → case Personnel) · admin/devices, admin/appConfig, admin/fleetStats — all admin-gated server-side (section 08).

Auth

Base44 OAuth in a system browser tab → token in the platform keystore (never localStorage-equivalent) → biometric unlock. Volunteer onboarding reuses the WhatsApp OTP flow. Guest links stay web.

Offline & sync

Encrypted SQLite mirroring the case bundle, an idempotent outbox queue, background media uploads, offline map tiles, and a per-type conflict policy — the full architecture is section 10.

PII discipline: encrypted at rest (iOS Data Protection / Android EncryptedFile), cache purged on case close and on remote sign-out, no subject data in notification payloads (push carries IDs; the app fetches content after unlock).

10 · Offline-first

Offline is the default, not the fallback

Searches happen in wadis, forests, and basement levels — assume zero connectivity as the normal case and treat the network as an optimization. Concretely: every read the volunteer needs mid-search is answered from a local database, and every write is accepted locally first, then delivered by a sync engine that never loses data and never lies about what has been delivered.

Conflict policy — decided per data type, not per incident

Data typePolicy
Log entries, evidence, audio, pings, check-insAppend-only — no conflicts possible. Every row is a new fact with device timestamp + server receive time; merging is trivial. This is why mobile writes are deliberately append-heavy.

Media, honesty, and hygiene

Media pipeline

Photos and audio land in the app sandbox (encrypted at rest), get an outbox row, and upload via the OS background transfer service (URLSession background / WorkManager) — surviving app kills and continuing on cell or Wi-Fi. The record links to the file only after the server confirms.

Truthful UI

The Deck bar's sync hairline plus a per-item state chip (ממתין לסנכרון · נשלח) on anything queued. A cold offline start shows the bundle age: "נכון ל־03:12". The app never shows server-fresh styling on stale data.

Hygiene & security

SQLCipher key behind biometric in Keychain/Keystore. Case data purged on case close, sign-out, and remote revoke. Storage budget: bundle ~2–5 MB, tiles 30–80 MB, media until confirmed — closed cases evicted automatically.

The degraded-mode ladder

Level 1

Online

Everything live; bundle refreshes silently in the background. AI analysis, plate lookup, broadcasts — the full app.

Level 2

Flaky signal

Reads stay local-first; writes queue and drain opportunistically. The user notices nothing except the hairline flickering.

Level 3

Fully offline

Complete core loop still works: subject card, my task, sector map, capture, report. Server-side features (AI, lookups, sending broadcasts) are visibly disabled, not broken.

Level 4

Cold start offline

App killed, no signal, reopened in the field: last bundle loads instantly with its age banner. The volunteer still knows who they are looking for and where.

Acceptance test: airplane mode for a full simulated search — check in, navigate, photograph two findings, voice-report, complete the task — then signal restored: everything reaches the חמ״ל in order, exactly once, with correct timestamps. This test gates the MVP release.

12 · Risks & open questions

What can bite, and the mitigation

RiskMitigation
Base44 SDK on React NativeThe SDK targets browsers; token flow and storage assumptions must be spiked in week 1. Fallback is a thin authenticated fetch wrapper — the function/entity REST surface is simple.
Background location policyApple and Google both scrutinize background GPS. Session-scoped tracking with explicit per-event opt-in, visible indicator, and auto-stop is exactly the pattern they approve. Write the store-review justification early.
PII on lost devicesEncrypted storage, biometric gate, cache purge on case close, server-side device revocation (DeviceToken.revoked) forcing sign-out.
Offline conflictsKeep mobile writes append-heavy (log, evidence, pings, status transitions); field-level editing stays connected-only in v1. Per-type policy in section 10; the airplane-mode acceptance test gates the MVP.
case_id casing in cached queriesThe historical mixed-casing trap applies to the local store too — normalize with caseIdVariants() at the sync layer, once.
Two clients driftingMobile consumes existing functions only; any new logic lands in base44/functions, never duplicated client-side. Same rule the web app already follows.
Critical alerts entitlementiOS critical alerts need Apple approval — SAR is a textbook qualifying use case, but apply at Phase 0, not Phase 2.
